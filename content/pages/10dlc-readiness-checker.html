10DLC Readiness Checker

Twenty-two yes-or-no questions covering everything a campaign reviewer actually checks. Answer honestly and you will see, before you spend a vetting fee, which items would get your brand or campaign rejected — and what to fix first. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

How the score is built

Every question maps to a reason campaigns are rejected in practice. Items marked critical are automatic rejections on their own — a mismatched legal name, a missing privacy-policy sentence, sample messages without opt-out language. Items marked important lower your trust score or throughput rather than failing you outright. The checker weights them accordingly, so a single critical miss shows as "not ready" even if everything else is perfect. That is how reviewers treat it too.

What reviewers are really looking for

Carrier vetting partners are not evaluating whether your business is legitimate in some general sense. They are checking that a set of records is complete and internally consistent: the legal name matches the IRS letter, the address matches the state filing, the website domain matches the email domain and the links in your samples, the use case matches the samples, and the opt-in you describe is something they can see evidence of. Most rejections are not fraud calls. They are contradictions between two fields.

The three things people get wrong most

  • The privacy policy sentence. Reviewers look for an explicit statement that mobile numbers and SMS consent are not shared with third parties or affiliates for marketing. A generic policy without that sentence fails.
  • Opt-in you cannot show. "Customers agree when they sign up" is not evidence. A screenshot of the form with the SMS disclosure beside the phone field, or the keyword flow, is.
  • Samples that do not match the use case. Register a transactional campaign and include a promotional sample, and the campaign is rejected on that alone.
