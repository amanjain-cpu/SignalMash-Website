Two products sold under one label

"AI chatbot for RCS" covers two different things, and the difference decides whether the bot is safe in front of customers.

‍

A flow bot is a decision tree of rich cards, carousels and suggested replies that the customer taps through. It cannot say anything you did not write. Every builder below can do this, and for a product launch, an appointment flow or a survey it is often all you need.

‍

A generative agent reads the customer's free-text question and writes an answer. This is where the value and the risk both live. An agent that answers from the open internet or from its training data will, sooner or later, invent a discount, a return policy or a delivery date. On a channel where every message goes out under your verified brand name, logo and checkmark, and where the carriers approved that sender for a declared use case, that is a sender-verification problem, not a cosmetic one.

‍

So the question for every vendor is not "do you have AI" but "what can the AI say, and what happens when it does not know".

The five questions that separate the builders

  1. Grounding. Does the agent answer only from knowledge you supplied and approved, or can it fall back to the model's general knowledge?
  2. Action boundary. Can the agent only trigger steps you authored (a carousel, a capture, a handoff), or can it improvise actions?
  3. Compliance layer. Are STOP, HELP and consent handled before the AI sees the message, so an opt-out is never a model judgement call? For US A2P traffic this is a TCPA question, not a feature.
  4. Handoff. When the agent is unsure, does the conversation land in a human inbox with the full thread, or does the customer hit a wall?
  5. Channel. Is RCS native, with a verified sender and automatic SMS fallback, or is RCS an integration you wire up separately from the AI product?

Pricing matters too, but only after those five, because a cheap agent that goes off-script costs more than any subscription.

What each builder is for

SimplyRCS by Signalmash: a generative agent that answers only from your content, on verified RCS, without engineers. The AI and the channel are one product. You describe the bot in plain English, say a product-launch bot with a carousel of bestsellers and an email capture, and the Assistant assembles the steps on the canvas: rich cards, carousels, tap menus, questions that save answers and human handoffs, every change reviewable and undoable. One click drafts a knowledge base from the bot you built, covering products, policies, prices and FAQs, and you keep, edit or reject each line. The agent then answers only from that approved knowledge and can only trigger steps you authored, so it cannot invent a discount, a policy or a button that does not exist. STOP, HELP and consent run before the AI ever sees a message; anything outside the boundary lands in the shared team inbox with the full thread, where a person can take over live and hand back. A sandbox lets you ask it the hard questions before publishing. It runs on your verified RCS agent with automatic SMS and MMS fallback. AI is metered as credits near cost, $20 a month for roughly 400 AI conversations or $90 for roughly 1,500, on top of published message rates of $0.0039 per RCS text segment and $0.0150 per card, carousel or media message, with no monthly platform or agent fee and a one-time $250 RCS Sender ID setup and review. Limits: US carriers only, and no 100-language NLU library; the agent works from your content in the languages it is written in.

How the handoff works, vendor by vendor

Seven of the eight document a live-agent path. What differs is where the human works and what arrives with the conversation. On SimplyRCS the handoff is a node in the same canvas as the cards and menus, the whole thread lands in the shared inbox, and STOP and HELP stay honoured mid-handoff. Infobip Answers documents live agent transfer; Sinch Chatlayer, handoff to a contact centre; Bird, a handoff to a human beside its Flow Builder; Quiq is built around its own digital contact centre; Vonage AI Studio documents routing to live agents from a flow. Klaviyo Customer Agent had no human handoff documented on its public pages when checked. On Twilio the handoff and the agent desk (Flex, or your own) are yours to build. The RCS-specific question to add: does the conversation stay inside the verified, branded thread after the human takes over, or drop to a separate tool and a bare number?
