Political and Non-Profit SMS and Voice Campaigns for the 2026 Midterms

Quick answer: what a political committee needs to text voters in 2026. To send SMS from a 10-digit number, a 527 political organisation needs a Campaign Verify authorization token, then a brand and campaign registered with The Campaign Registry under the Political use case. Verification and registration typically take 5 to 15 business days, and considerably longer if committee details do not match official filings exactly.

Political or charity? The distinction most organisations get wrong

10DLC treats political and charitable messaging as two separate special use cases, with two different verification paths.

  • 527 political organisations - candidate committees, PACs, Super PACs, party committees and ballot-measure committees - must be verified by Campaign Verify and supply the resulting authorization token before the Political use case can be unlocked.
  • 501(c)(3) non-profits register under the Charity use case and do not need a Campaign Verify token.

This matters more than it sounds. A 527 that registers without a Campaign Verify token does not simply get a slower approval. It lands in the lowest tier of 10DLC messaging limits and does not qualify for the Political use case at all. For a campaign planning a get-out-the-vote push, that is the difference between reaching your list and throttling on it.

Campaign Verify tokens also expire and need periodic renewal, so a token issued for an earlier cycle may no longer be valid for this one. Check before you assume you are covered.

What you need before you begin

Have all of this in hand before you start the application, because gaps are what send committees back to the end of the queue:

  • Your FEC or state filing ID
  • The committee name exactly as it appears on those filings
  • EIN and supporting documentation
  • Treasurer name and contact details
  • A live, working campaign website

The word doing the most work in that list is exactly. A committee registered federally as "Friends of Jane Doe for Congress" cannot register as "Jane Doe for Congress" and expect a match.

Why political registrations get rejected

The common rejection reasons are consistent and almost all avoidable:

  • The committee name does not match the official filing character for character
  • The wrong committee type was selected
  • The person applying is not listed on the official filing
  • EIN documentation is missing or does not match
  • The campaign website is down, parked, or does not exist yet

Every one of these puts you back at the start of a queue you do not have the calendar to re-enter.

Throughput: what you can actually send on Election Day

Approved political campaigns are not all given the same capacity. Published industry guidance puts the ranges at roughly 75 to 300+ messages per second on 10DLC, up to around 300 per second on toll-free, and 500+ per second on a short code.

The practical implication: if your plan involves a single large send in the final 48 hours, decide your number type during registration, not the week before. Moving from 10DLC to a toll-free number or a short code after approval means starting a new registration.

Consent rules still apply to political messaging

Political speech carries broad First Amendment protection, but the TCPA regulates the technology, not the viewpoint. Autodialed and pre-recorded calls and texts to mobile numbers require prior express consent regardless of how political the content is. Opt-outs must be honoured promptly, and purchased or scraped lists are the fastest route to complaints, carrier filtering and a damaged sender reputation.

How long does political 10DLC registration take in 2026?

Do political campaigns need a Campaign Verify token?

Yes, if you are a 527 political organisation. Candidate committees, PACs, Super PACs, party committees and ballot-measure committees all need a Campaign Verify authorization token to unlock the Political special use case in 10DLC. Without one you are placed in the lowest messaging tier. 501(c)(3) non-profits are different - they register under the Charity use case and do not need a token.

Can non-profits text supporters without Campaign Verify?

A 501(c)(3) can register under the Charity special use case without a Campaign Verify token. The distinction is the organisation's tax status, not the subject matter of the messages, so an advocacy organisation should confirm which category it actually falls into before registering.

Why was my political campaign registration rejected?

The most common causes are a committee name that does not match the FEC or state filing exactly, the wrong committee type, an applicant who is not listed on the official filing, missing or mismatched EIN documentation, and a campaign website that is not live. Each of these requires resubmission, so verify all five before you apply rather than after.

Does the TCPA apply to political text messages?
