Twilio Competitors: 12 CPaaS Alternatives Compared (2026)

Quick answer: who are Twilio's main competitors?

Twilio's main CPaaS competitors in 2026 are Telnyx, Bandwidth, Plivo and SignalWire (developer-first platforms that undercut Twilio's published rates), Sinch, Infobip and Vonage (global enterprise omnichannel), Bird, Gupshup and Webex Connect (regional or ecosystem specialists), RingCentral and Podium (finished applications rather than APIs) and Signalmash (US carrier-grade CPaaS with flat published rates, done-for-you 10DLC and RCS registration, and engineer-staffed support). On published US list prices in September 2026 every developer-first competitor is cheaper than Twilio per SMS segment ($0.0039 to $0.0077 against $0.0083) and per outbound voice minute ($0.005 to $0.0080 against $0.0100). The right one depends on whether your pain is price, support, compliance paperwork or global reach.

All rates in this article are each provider's own published US list price, read on 3 and 4 September 2026. Carrier pass-through fees on messaging (roughly $0.0035 to $0.005 per outbound segment) are set by the carriers and identical whoever you buy from. Providers change prices and offer committed-use discounts; confirm on their pricing pages before deciding.

What a CPaaS is, in one paragraph

A communications platform as a service exposes telecom capabilities (SMS, MMS, RCS, voice calls, phone numbers, verification) as APIs so a developer can add them to an application without building telecom infrastructure. Uber's drivers and riders texting each other inside the app, a bank's one-time passcodes, a clinic's appointment reminders: all CPaaS. Twilio built the category and remains the largest provider, which is why every alternative gets measured against it. That does not make it the best fit for every buyer in 2026, and its published rates are now the highest on this list.

The 12 Twilio competitors

1. Telnyx

Owns a private global IP network and is a licensed US carrier, which is how it publishes the lowest developer-first rates on the list: $0.004 per SMS part, $0.005 per outbound voice minute, $1.00 numbers, no platform fee, and RCS on the same Messaging API since July 2025. The developer experience is excellent. Registration, deliverability troubleshooting and carrier escalation are yours to own, and support beyond tickets and chat is a paid tier. Best for engineering teams optimising unit cost, including internationally.

2. Bandwidth

A Tier-1 US carrier in its own right and the network several other CPaaS providers, Twilio included, ride on for US delivery. Published SMS at $0.006 on 10DLC; voice and enterprise pricing are contracted with minimum commitments. Best for enterprises and platforms sending tens of millions of messages or minutes that want a carrier relationship and an account team rather than a self-serve API startup.

3. Plivo

Founded by ex-Twilio engineers with an API deliberately close to Twilio's, which makes it the easiest migration. Published SMS at $0.0077 per segment, voice around $0.0055 per minute, and the cheapest long-code rental at $0.50 alongside Signalmash's Scale plan. Recently added an AI agent builder. Best for teams that want a Twilio-shaped toolkit at a modest discount with minimal code changes.

4. SignalWire

Built by the FreeSWITCH team. Published SMS at $0.00415 per segment on 10DLC, MMS $0.02 outbound, voice $0.0080 out and $0.0066 in, local numbers $0.50, and an AI Agent runtime at $0.16 a minute that bundles speech-to-text, an LLM and text-to-speech. Capacity above one call per second is metered ($15 to $40 per CPS per month) and 10DLC registration is self-service with fees passed through. Best for developers building voice applications, AI receptionists or video products from primitives.

5. Sinch

A global CPaaS assembled through acquisitions (Inteliquent, MessageMedia, Pathwire), operating in 180-plus countries with one of the strongest RCS practices and a close Google partnership. Pricing is quoted through sales. Best for mid-market and enterprise brands that need SMS, RCS, WhatsApp, email and voice under one contract with enterprise SLAs.

6. Infobip

One of the largest direct-to-operator networks in the world, spanning 60-plus countries, with polished marketing tooling and the most-cited RCS adoption research (Messaging Trends 2026). Quote-based. Best for enterprises with significant European, Asian or Latin American audiences that want one vendor for global messaging.

7. Vonage

Now owned by Ericsson, formerly Nexmo. Mature omnichannel APIs (SMS, RCS, voice, video, verify, WhatsApp) with premium enterprise support; US per-message rates are not published and third-party comparisons put voice near $0.0139 a minute. Best for enterprises that want deep cross-channel orchestration with a premium support relationship, not for anyone optimising unit cost.

8. Bird (formerly MessageBird)

Rebranded in February 2024 around a combined marketing, CDP and conversational AI stack rather than standalone APIs. Strong in Europe, with a Flow Builder for automating replies and processes. Best for marketing teams, particularly outside the US, that want automation built in.

9. Gupshup

Massive scale in India and Southeast Asia, listed in Google's RCS for Business partner directory, with strong chatbot tooling layered on messaging. US carrier relationships are less direct than the US-focused providers. Best for businesses with a large non-US audience that need a single provider across regions.

10. Webex Connect (Cisco)

Cisco's CPaaS, built on the IMImobile acquisition, with a low-code journey builder and a place on Google's RCS partner directory. Its edge is procurement: at enterprises where Cisco is already an approved vendor, the buying cycle collapses. Enterprise-only in practice.

11. RingCentral and Podium

Not APIs but finished products. RingCentral is a cloud phone system with messaging, video and a contact centre sold per seat; Podium is a customer-engagement and texting application for local businesses. Consider them if you want a phone system or a texting inbox rather than a platform to build on. If you already run one and want cheaper minutes underneath it, that is a SIP trunking conversation.

12. Signalmash

A US carrier-grade CPaaS with direct AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile and Bandwidth interconnects. Published rates: SMS $0.0039 per segment on the no-minimum Launch plan ($0.0035 Growth, $0.0031 Scale), MMS $0.0159, voice $0.0068 outbound and $0.0053 inbound, numbers from $0.95 down to $0.50, ten calls per second included, plus a $19 monthly platform fee. What is different is the operating model: Signalmash completes 10DLC, toll-free and RCS registration for customers as part of onboarding, provisions E911 and CNAM, and runs support through a shared Slack or Teams channel with an engineer in it. RCS business messaging comes through SimplyRCS, with a no-code campaign builder, verified sender branding and automatic SMS fallback. Coverage is US and Canada. Best for businesses that need messaging and voice to work without a telecom specialist on staff, and for teams that have been stuck in a carrier approval queue. Rates are on the pricing page; the API is documented on the SMS API and OTP SMS API pages.

What happened to Zipwhip?

Zipwhip used to appear on lists like this one and no longer belongs on them. Twilio acquired Zipwhip in 2021 for $850 million and shut the Zipwhip product down on 1 December 2023, keeping the carrier connections and landline-texting technology. Roughly 30,000 businesses had to migrate. If you landed here looking for a Zipwhip replacement, every provider above is one; Signalmash and Plivo are the two most often chosen by former Zipwhip customers for landline texting.

How to choose between them

  • Your pain is the bill and you have engineers: Telnyx, then Bandwidth at scale. Run the arithmetic with carrier fees included, because the headline gap halves once they are added.
  • Your pain is support or a stuck 10DLC registration: Signalmash. The registration is done for you and a rejection does not become your project.
  • You are building a voice or AI-agent product: SignalWire, or Twilio if you are already deep in its ecosystem.
  • You need many countries and one contract: Sinch or Infobip.
  • You want the least code change from Twilio: Plivo.
  • You want a phone system or texting app, not an API: RingCentral or Podium.

Before switching, price your real monthly mix (segments, MMS share, minutes by direction, number count, carrier split) with two or three providers, ask who does the 10DLC registration and what a rejection costs, ask what happens at 2am when delivery drops, and confirm you can port your numbers out again. People also ask

Who is Twilio's biggest competitor?

By scale, Sinch and Infobip are Twilio's largest competitors globally, with Vonage (Ericsson) close behind. In the US developer market the most direct rivals on price are Telnyx, Bandwidth, Plivo and SignalWire, all of which publish per-segment SMS rates below Twilio's $0.0083. Signalmash competes on managed compliance and support rather than scale.

What companies are like Twilio?

Companies offering the same programmable SMS, voice and number APIs include Telnyx, Bandwidth, Plivo, SignalWire, Sinch, Infobip, Vonage, Bird, Gupshup, Webex Connect and Signalmash. RingCentral and Podium are often listed alongside them but sell finished communication products rather than developer APIs.

Is there a cheaper alternative to Twilio?

Yes. On published September 2026 US list rates, Signalmash ($0.0039 per SMS segment on its no-minimum plan, with a $19 monthly platform fee), Telnyx ($0.004), SignalWire ($0.00415), Bandwidth ($0.006) and Plivo ($0.0077) are all below Twilio's $0.0083, and Telnyx ($0.005) and Signalmash ($0.0068) are well below Twilio's $0.0100 per outbound voice minute. Carrier pass-through fees are identical on every provider, so compare all-in costs for your real volume.

Which Twilio competitor is best for 10DLC registration?

Most developer-first competitors (Telnyx, Plivo, SignalWire, Twilio itself) leave 10DLC brand and campaign registration to you through a self-service portal and charge the registry fees again on a resubmission. Signalmash completes the registration on the customer's behalf as part of onboarding and manages resubmissions; Bandwidth provides an account team for enterprise customers.

Can I move my phone numbers from Twilio to a competitor?

Yes. US local and toll-free numbers port between providers with a letter of authorisation and a recent invoice, typically in one to two weeks for local numbers and two to four for toll-free. Your 10DLC campaigns and any verified RCS agent do not travel with the number and must be re-filed with the new provider.
