Twilio Elastic SIP Trunking Alternatives: 5 Providers Compared (2026)

Quick answer: the best Twilio Elastic SIP Trunking alternatives

Twilio Elastic SIP Trunking is reliable but expensive at list price ($0.0100 per minute outbound to the US 48, $0.0034 inbound, $1.15 per number, paid support plans). The main alternatives in 2026 are Signalmash (direct Tier-1 routes, $0.0068/min outbound, engineer support on Slack, SMS and RCS on the same numbers), Telnyx ($0.005/min on its own network), Bandwidth (Tier-1 carrier, contract pricing), Plivo and SignalWire. Switching is a matter of pointing your PBX or SBC at a new trunk and porting numbers; most businesses cut their voice bill by 30 to 50 percent.

This article is part of the Signalmash SIP trunking resource series: what SIP trunking is, the best providers in 2026, what it costs, wholesale SIP for resellers and Twilio Elastic SIP alternatives.

Twilio invented the phrase "elastic SIP trunking" and, for teams already writing code against its API, staying put is often the right call. But if you are running a PBX or a contact center rather than a programmable-voice application, you are paying a premium for tooling you do not use. Here is what Twilio does well, where the bill hurts, and how the alternatives compare.

What Twilio Elastic SIP Trunking is

Elastic SIP trunking is Twilio's name for a SIP trunk with no fixed channel count: capacity scales with your calls and you pay per minute. The product includes unlimited concurrent calls, secure trunking (TLS and SRTP), global termination, a strong console and API, call recording and SIP Insights analytics. It integrates cleanly with the rest of Twilio, which is the main reason to choose it.

Where the Twilio bill hurts

  • Outbound rate. $0.0100 per minute to the lower 48 is roughly double Telnyx and 45 percent above Signalmash list rates.
  • Add-ons. E911 at $0.75 per number per month, recording at $0.0025 per minute plus storage, SIP Insights at $0.0024 per minute; these add up quickly on a contact center.
  • Support. Free support is email only; developer, business and personalized plans are priced as a percentage of spend.
  • Aggregated routes. Twilio is not a Tier-1 carrier; calls transit partner networks, and when audio quality drops you are debugging through Twilio rather than with the carrier that owns the route.
  • Messaging complexity. Twilio's 10DLC registration, toll-free verification and RCS are self-service; if the same numbers need texting, someone on your team owns the filings.

Alternative 2: Telnyx

Choose if: you are a developer team and want the lowest list rate with a global footprint.

Telnyx owns its network and is a licensed US carrier: about $0.005 outbound and $0.0032 inbound per minute, numbers $1.00 per month, a Twilio-style API and portal, and broad international coverage. Support is ticket and chat based, with enterprise support as a paid tier.

Alternative 3: Bandwidth

Choose if: you send tens of millions of minutes and want a Tier-1 carrier on contract.

Bandwidth is one of the networks Twilio itself uses. Pricing is negotiated (third-party comparisons put outbound at $0.0035 to $0.008 per minute), with minimum commitments and enterprise onboarding. Not designed for a self-serve PBX migration.

Alternative 4: Plivo

Choose if: you want a Twilio-like API toolkit at lower rates.

Plivo's Zentrunk SIP lists around $0.0055 per minute each way with numbers at $0.80 per month and a familiar console. Support beyond tickets is a paid add-on.

Alternative 5: SignalWire

Choose if: you build on FreeSWITCH or Asterisk and want programmable media at low cost.

SignalWire lists about $0.0055 outbound and $0.0050 inbound with numbers at $0.85 per month, from the team that maintains FreeSWITCH. Excellent for engineers building voice applications; lighter on managed support.

What switching involves

  1. Inventory: list your numbers, monthly minutes by direction, peak concurrent calls and the PBX or SBC you use.
  2. Trial trunk: most providers, including Signalmash, let you run a test trunk with a few numbers alongside Twilio before moving anything.
  3. Port numbers: submit a port request with a letter of authorization and a recent Twilio invoice; local numbers port in 1 to 2 weeks, toll-free in 2 to 4.
  4. Cut over outbound first: point outbound routes at the new trunk while inbound still lands on Twilio, then port inbound.
  5. Re-provision E911 and CNAM on the new carrier before the port completes.
  6. Close the Twilio trunk once all numbers have moved, and check for orphaned numbers still billing.

When to stay on Twilio

If your application uses TwiML, Programmable Voice, Flex or Studio and the SIP trunk is just one input to that stack, the integration value outweighs the per-minute premium. Switch when the trunk is the product: a PBX, a contact center platform, Teams Direct Routing or a dialer where minutes are most of the bill.

People also ask

What is the cheapest alternative to Twilio SIP trunking?

On published US list rates Telnyx is cheapest at about $0.005 per minute outbound, followed by Plivo, SignalWire and Signalmash at $0.0055 to $0.0068. Twilio lists $0.0100. Bandwidth can be cheaper still on a volume contract. Compare inbound rates, number fees, E911 and support costs too, since those can reverse the ranking.

Is Telnyx better than Twilio for SIP trunking?

Telnyx is cheaper and runs its own network, which many teams prefer for call quality and cost. Twilio has the larger ecosystem and better integration with its programmable voice products. For a plain SIP trunk feeding a PBX or contact center, Telnyx or Signalmash usually delivers the same quality at a lower price.

Can I move my phone numbers from Twilio to another SIP provider?

Yes. Any US local or toll-free number can be ported. You submit a port request to the new provider with a letter of authorization and a recent Twilio invoice; local numbers typically move in one to two weeks and toll-free numbers in two to four. Signalmash handles the port and re-provisions E911 and CNAM as part of onboarding.

What does elastic SIP trunking mean?

Elastic SIP trunking is a SIP trunk with no fixed channel count: capacity expands and contracts with your call volume and you pay per minute rather than per channel. Twilio popularised the term, but Signalmash, Telnyx and others offer the same model.
