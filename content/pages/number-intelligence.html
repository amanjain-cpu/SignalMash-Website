Phone number intelligence (also called number lookup, phone intelligence or line-type lookup) is a real-time query against carrier and registry databases that returns what is known about a telephone number before you call or text it. Four places a lookup pays for itself

Before a 10DLC or short code campaign. Consumer lists collected over years are typically 15 to 30 percent landline or disconnected. Every SMS to a landline is billed and never delivered, and a high failure rate on a new 10DLC campaign looks like spam to carrier filters. A line-type pass over the list before the first send removes both problems and pays for itself on the first campaign of any size.

At sign-up and checkout. A number that is VoIP, was ported in the last few days, or whose CNAM does not resemble the name on the form is not proof of fraud, but it is the cheapest risk signal you can add to an OTP flow: step up to a voice OTP or a manual review for the small share of sign-ups that trip the rules, and leave everyone else alone.

In the contact center. CNAM and carrier data on an inbound call let the agent see who is calling and confirm the caller's carrier matches the account on file, which shortens verification and catches social-engineering attempts that use a spoofed display number.

Before an outbound voice campaign. Federal DNC status plus line type tells the dialler which numbers may be called with an automated system at all, and which need a manual dial or should be skipped, before the campaign starts rather than after the complaint arrives.

What is phone number intelligence?

A real-time lookup that returns what carrier databases know about a phone number: line type (mobile, landline, VoIP, toll-free), the current carrier after porting, the registered caller name (CNAM) and, in the US, whether the number is on the federal Do Not Call list. It is used to clean lists, choose routes, verify identities and stay compliant before a message or call is sent.

Can a number lookup tell whether a number is VoIP or a cell phone?

Yes. Line type is derived from the carrier and number-block records, which identify wireless carriers, wireline carriers and VoIP providers. Numbers behind VoIP apps and virtual-number services are classified as VoIP, which is why many sign-up flows treat them as higher risk and ask for a second verification step.

Is a DNC check enough for TCPA compliance?

No. The National Do Not Call Registry governs telemarketing calls and texts to listed numbers, but the TCPA separately requires prior express written consent for marketing messages sent with an automated system, honouring company-specific opt-outs, calling-hour limits and, for texts, the 10DLC registration carriers require. Treat the DNC scrub as one mandatory step in a documented consent process and keep the check date with each record.

How accurate is carrier lookup for ported numbers?

Very accurate for US and Canadian numbers, because ports are recorded in the Number Portability Administration Center as they complete and lookups query that data rather than the original block owner. The exception is the first minutes after a port, and numbers outside the US and Canada, where data quality varies by country.
