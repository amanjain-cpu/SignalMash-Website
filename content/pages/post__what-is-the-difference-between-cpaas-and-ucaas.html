What Is the Difference Between CPaaS and UCaaS?

The Differences Between CPaaS and UCaaS Explained

The need for greater flexibility in the modern enterprise has been growing steadily in recent years. So has the need to adopt cloud-based communications to support frontline employees, contact center agents, and knowledge workers in their communications outside and inside the office. The two models at the center of this adoption are communications platform as a service (CPaaS) and unified communications as a service (UCaaS). While both platforms provide communications functionality, they are intended for different purposes.

What is CPaaS?

Communications platform as a service (CPaaS) is a cloud-based platform that enables enterprises to develop, deploy, and manage custom communications applications and workflows. CPaaS provides an API and SDK that allow developers to create applications that can be integrated into their existing software and workflows. These applications can include programmatic voice, messaging, chatbots, and more.

What is UCaaS?

Unified communications as a service (UCaaS) is a cloud-based platform that provides businesses with a comprehensive suite of communications and collaboration tools. UCaaS includes VoIP, video conferencing, instant messaging, presence management, and more.

CPaaS and UCaaS in the Modern Workplace

The modern workplace is increasingly mobile and distributed, with employees working from different locations and on different devices. This has created a need for communications solutions that are more flexible and easier to use. CPaaS and UCaaS platforms are designed to meet these needs by allowing businesses to develop custom communications workflows using their own applications, workflows and software.

CPaaS is typically used to bring communications functionality, such as messaging and programmatic voice, to applications. UCaaS revolves around more comprehensive communications and collaboration, such as video conferencing.

What are the Benefits of CPaaS?

CPaaS provides businesses with several benefits including:

  • The ability to develop custom communications functionality that can be integrated into existing applications workflows.
  • A wide range of features such as real-time voice and video calling, messaging and chatbots into an existing software through an API.
  • Greater flexibility in the way businesses communicate with customers and employees.
  • Lower costs associated with deploying and managing communications infrastructure.

What are the Benefits of UCaaS?

UCaaS provides businesses with an array of benefits that include:

  • A comprehensive suite of communications and collaboration tools.
  • Increased productivity through better collaboration and communications.
  • Lower costs associated with deploying and managing communications infrastructure.

Differences Between CPaaS and UCaaS

The main difference between CPaaS and UCaaS is the focus of each platform. CPaaS is designed to bring communications to existing applications, while UCaaS is a comprehensive communications solution like Zoom, Google Meet and Teams.

CPaaS provides businesses with the ability to incorporate communications functionality into their existing applications. With a CPaaS, companies have access to real-time voice and video calling, messaging, and more.

UCaaS provides businesses with a comprehensive suite of communications and collaboration tools. The platform includes VoIP, video conferencing, instant messaging, presence management, and more.

CPaaS Gives Software Developers Complete Control over How Their Applications Communicate

Unlike UCaaS, a CPaaS platform does not come in pre-built versions. Instead, they allow developers to integrate real-time communications capabilities into their applications.

For example, if you’re in the health sector, you might develop a fitness app that features standard as well as individualized training sessions. Enabling CPaaS on your app could mean adding messaging and voice calling. Users don’t need to leave your fitness app to message their personal trainers; instead, they may do so right within the app.
