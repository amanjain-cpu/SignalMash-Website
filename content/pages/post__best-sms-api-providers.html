Best SMS API Providers in 2026: Published US Rates, Compared Honestly

Quick answer: which SMS API provider is cheapest, and does it matter

On published US list rates in September 2026, Signalmash and Telnyx are the cheapest: $0.0039 per segment on Signalmash's no-minimum plan and $0.004 per part on Telnyx, followed by Bandwidth at $0.006 for 10DLC, Plivo at $0.0077 and Twilio at $0.0083. Vonage does not publish per-message US rates at all and asks you to talk to sales. But the headline rate decides less of your bill than people expect: carrier pass-through fees, phone-number rental, platform fees, 10DLC registration and vetting, segment counting and failed-message charges are all billed separately, and on a typical low-to-mid-volume US programme they add up to more than the base rate. The honest way to choose is to price your own message mix across two or three providers, and then weigh what happens when something breaks.

Why the headline rate is not what you pay

Every provider on that list bills these separately, and none of them are optional:

  • Carrier pass-through fees. AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon and US Cellular each levy a per-message fee on A2P traffic. Twilio publishes roughly $0.0035 extra for AT&T long-code outbound and $0.0045 for T-Mobile, for example. On a $0.004 base rate, the carrier fee can cost more than the message.
  • Segments, not messages. Billing is per 160-character GSM segment (70 if you use an emoji or a smart quote, which switches the encoding to UCS-2). A 200-character message is two segments everywhere. Providers who quote "per message" and providers who quote "per part" or "per segment" are quoting the same thing. The SMS character counter shows the segment count for any draft.
  • Number rental and platform fees. $0.50 to $2.15 per number per month, and some providers add a flat monthly platform fee (Signalmash charges $19 on its pay-as-you-go plans). Irrelevant for one number at volume, material if your use case needs fifty numbers or sends a few hundred messages a month.
  • 10DLC registration and vetting. One-time brand and vetting fees plus a monthly campaign fee, largely pass-through from The Campaign Registry, but providers differ on whether they add a margin and whether a rejected resubmission is charged again. Our A2P 10DLC requirements guide breaks down the three cost layers.
  • Failure charges. Some providers bill a small fee on failed messages. Twilio publishes $0.001 per failed message.

Put together, a business sending 50,000 segments a month on a handful of numbers will often find the gap between a $0.004 provider and a $0.0083 provider is a fraction of what the rate card implies, because the carrier fees are identical for both.

The providers, briefly

Twilio

The default choice and the biggest ecosystem: the most documentation, the most tutorials, the most third-party integrations, and a hire-anyone-who-knows-it advantage. It is also the most expensive published rate on this list, support is tiered and paid at the levels most businesses need, and 10DLC registration is your job. Good fit when engineering capacity is plentiful and vendor familiarity matters more than unit cost.

Telnyx

One of the two cheapest published rates, on a private IP network with a genuinely good developer experience and no platform fee. The trade-off is the same as Twilio's: self-service. If your team is comfortable owning registration, deliverability troubleshooting and carrier escalations, the rate is hard to beat.

Plivo

Priced between Telnyx and Twilio with a similar developer-first model, and the cheapest long-code number rental alongside Signalmash's Scale plan at $0.50 a month. Worth pricing if you need many numbers.

How to run a fair comparison

  1. Price your actual message mix. Take a real month: segment count, carrier split if you know it, MMS share, number count. Ask each provider to price that, carrier fees included.
  2. Ask for the all-in number. Base rate plus carrier pass-through plus number rental plus platform fee plus 10DLC monthly. A quote without carrier fees is not a quote.
  3. Ask who does the 10DLC registration and what a rejection costs. This is where self-service providers and managed providers genuinely diverge.
  4. Ask what happens at 2am when delivery rates drop. Who answers, how fast, and can they escalate to the carrier?
  5. Check the exit. Can you port your numbers out, and how long does it take? A cheap rate on numbers you cannot move is not cheap.
  6. Test deliverability before you commit. Send real traffic to real handsets on all four major carriers for a week. Published rates are comparable; delivery rates are not.

Which SMS API is cheapest?

On published US list rates in September 2026, Signalmash ($0.0039 per segment on its no-minimum plan, with a $19 monthly platform fee) and Telnyx ($0.004 per part, no platform fee) are cheapest, followed by Bandwidth at $0.006 for 10DLC traffic, Plivo at $0.0077 and Twilio at $0.0083. Vonage, Sinch and Infobip do not publish US per-message rates. Carrier pass-through fees are charged on top by every provider and are identical regardless of who you buy through, which narrows the real difference considerably.

How much does an SMS API cost per message in the US?

Base rates run from about $0.0039 to $0.0083 per segment for standard A2P traffic. On top of that you pay carrier pass-through fees of roughly $0.003 to $0.005 per message depending on the destination carrier, phone-number rental of $0.50 to $2.15 per number per month, any platform fee, and 10DLC registration and campaign fees. Budget the all-in figure rather than the base rate.

What is the difference between Twilio and Telnyx?

Both are developer-first, self-service SMS APIs. Telnyx publishes a materially lower base rate ($0.004 versus $0.0083 per part) and runs its own private IP network; Twilio has the larger ecosystem, more documentation and integrations, and greater familiarity in the hiring market. Neither handles 10DLC registration for you as standard.

Do I need 10DLC registration whichever SMS API I choose?

Yes. 10DLC registration is a carrier requirement for application-to-person SMS on US long codes, not a provider feature, so it applies no matter whose API you use. What differs is who does the work: some providers hand you a portal, others complete the brand and campaign registration on your behalf and manage resubmissions.

Does a cheaper SMS API mean worse deliverability?

Not inherently — price mostly reflects margin and support model rather than route quality, and several of the cheapest providers own their own network. What does affect deliverability is registration quality, content consistency with your registered samples, and how quickly someone can escalate to a carrier when filtering starts. Test with real traffic on all four major carriers before committing.
