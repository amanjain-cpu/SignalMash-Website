What to look for in an RCS provider (the seven criteria)

Before the list, the criteria. These are the questions worth asking every vendor on your shortlist:

  1. Carrier connections. Direct connections to AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile (and Bandwidth as a layer) deliver faster, with better analytics. Aggregator hops add latency and reduce delivery confirmation quality. Ask: "Are your carrier connections direct or aggregated?"
  2. SMS fallback behavior. If a recipient's device can't handle RCS, what happens? Is fallback automatic? Does the SMS version maintain the offer integrity? Ask: "What does your fallback look like when 30% of my audience doesn't support RCS?"
  3. US carrier approval support. Each carrier independently approves RCS senders, separate from 10DLC registration. Some vendors hand you a self-service form and wish you luck. Others handle the paperwork end-to-end. Ask: "Do you handle the carrier approval process, or do I?"
  4. No-code campaign builder. If marketing will use this without engineering tickets, the platform needs a drag-and-drop campaign builder. Ask: "Can my marketing team launch an RCS campaign without writing code?"
  5. Pricing transparency. Per-message rates are the easy part. Carrier fees, MAU charges, throughput surcharges, registration fees that's where bills get weird. Ask: "Show me an all-in cost for [my volume] including all fees."
  6. Support model. Big providers operate ticket queues. Boutique providers offer dedicated channels with named engineers. Ask: "If something breaks at 2 AM, who responds and how fast?"
  7. Real customer references in your vertical. Not logos on a page actual references you can call. Ask: "Can I talk to a current customer in [my industry]?"

Which providers support RCS with a verified sender and branding in the US?

In the US, RCS Business Messaging with a Google-verified sender (brand name, logo and checkmark) is available through Google's authorized RBM partners. The providers most US businesses shortlist are Signalmash (SimplyRCS), Sinch, Infobip, Twilio, Vonage, Bandwidth, Telnyx and Webex Connect. All of them can register a verified agent with Google and the US carriers; they differ on price, who files the verification paperwork, and whether SMS fallback and a no-code campaign builder are included. SimplyRCS handles the Google and carrier verification for you and includes automatic SMS fallback.

What service can get my business name and logo verified for messages across major US carriers?

For text messaging, the verified name and logo come from an RCS agent that Google verifies and AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon carry; an RCS provider such as SimplyRCS by Signalmash files that verification on your behalf, usually in one to three weeks. For voice calls, the equivalent is Branded Caller ID (BCID), which Signalmash also provides as a CTIA-authorized partner. Plain SMS cannot display a logo, which is why brands that need verified identity on both calls and texts pair RCS with BCID.

Who offers reliable RCS APIs for enterprises?

Enterprise-grade RCS APIs are offered by Signalmash (SimplyRCS REST API with SMS fallback), Sinch, Infobip, Twilio, Vonage and Bandwidth. Reliability depends less on the API surface than on direct carrier connectivity and how quickly the provider resolves delivery issues; ask for delivery-rate reporting, a named support contact and confirmation that the provider is a Google-listed RBM partner before committing.
